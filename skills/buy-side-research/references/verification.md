# Verification discipline

How evidence is handled on every run — and how to split the work when the
research is decomposed. Applies whether one researcher works the phases in
sequence or workstreams are delegated; nothing here assumes parallel execution.

---

## Premises are claims

The brief, a prior note, a screen, or a remembered fact is a **claim to
verify, not a fact to build on** — including the premise of the assignment
itself. The first research act on any inherited claim is to check the primary
record for the event that supposedly happened: a stock that "fell on trial
data" may turn out to have fallen on something else entirely, and the filing
that should exist for the claimed event may not exist at all.

When writing a workstream brief from inherited material, phrase it as
*"reportedly X — confirm what actually happened"*, never as *"given X"*. The
wording decides whether the premise gets attacked or inherited.

## Tag everything

Every finding is **VERIFIED** (primary source, dated, cited) or
**UNVERIFIED** (secondary only, or inference). State gaps explicitly —
*"not obtainable this session"* — rather than filling them with plausible
numbers. A confident figure with no tag is treated as unverified.

## Decisive facts need two trails

For the one date or number the decision hangs on (a patent settlement date, a
covenant, a deal term), require **two independent source trails** before
treating it as settled. Independent means different underlying sources — a
filing and a court docket — not two articles citing the same press release.
One trail is a lead; convergence is a fact.

## Decomposing the phases

The phases group naturally into workstreams that do not depend on each other's
conclusions:

| Workstream | Phases |
|---|---|
| Mandate / compliance | 0 |
| Business economics & industry | 5–7 |
| Moat, financial quality, management | 8–10 |
| Expectations & valuation inputs | 11–12 |
| Bear case, kill criteria, mispricing | 16, 17, 21 |

Run them in any order, or delegate them where the environment supports
working in parallel. Either way, **reverse valuation, scenarios, base rates,
portfolio fit, and the memo stay with whoever holds the full picture** —
they are synthesis, not collection, and they change as each workstream lands.

When workstreams are delegated: pass premises as claims (above); require the
tagging discipline in each brief; and treat any delivery as possibly partial —
confirm a workstream's full output arrived before synthesizing on it. Deliberately
assign the decisive fact to two workstreams; the redundancy is the verification.

## Reconcile anchor data before writing

Multi-source research produces contradictions. Before the memo, reconcile
every collision and record it in the memo's Anchor Data section — resolved
(which source won, and why) or explicitly unresolved. Common collisions:

- prices or market caps quoted from different dates
- "cash" with and without long-term investments
- "debt" that is actually operating-lease liabilities
- stale analyst price targets carried in from an older note
- event dates that differ across aggregators (check the filing date)

A contradiction that survives reconciliation is a finding: either a source is
wrong, or two definitions are being conflated — both are worth a line.
