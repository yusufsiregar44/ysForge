# Portfolio analysis

Step 7 of [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md), and the primary reference for ETF, diversification, geography and sector-exposure work.

A security must improve the portfolio, not merely look attractive in isolation.

---

## Portfolio role

Every investment needs an explicit role: core compounder, growth, value, income, diversifier, cyclical, tactical, hedge, or optionality. **If the role is unclear, reconsider ownership.**

## Concentration

Evaluate across single names, sectors, industries, factors, geographies, currencies, business models, and economic drivers.

Nominal diversification is insufficient — **twenty stocks can still represent one underlying trade.**

## Hidden correlation

> **What common variable could cause several positions to fail simultaneously?**

Candidates: interest rates, USD, oil, AI capex, consumer spending, semiconductor demand, Chinese growth, credit availability, government spending.

Group holdings by underlying economic driver, not by sector label.

## Diversification research

- Does this reduce dependence on an existing risk?
- Does it introduce a genuinely new return engine?
- What expected return are we accepting?
- What happens during stress?
- What currency, sector and factor exposure is added?
- What risk is being exchanged for the diversification?

Diversification should seek **attractive expected returns generated by economically distinct sources** — not merely more line items.

---

## Currency

Always separate the **trading currency**, the **listing currency**, and the **underlying economic currency**. The currency an ETF trades in does not determine its actual currency exposure.

Analyze revenue currencies, asset currencies, liability currencies, hedging, and the investor's own liability currency.

## ETF research

Research index methodology, holdings, sector and geographic concentration, currency exposure, top-10 concentration, factor exposure, valuation, turnover, fees, liquidity, domicile, distribution policy, Shariah methodology, and tax treatment where relevant.

> **What underlying economic exposure am I actually buying?**

## Geographic diversification

When comparing regions, analyze differences in sector composition, growth, margins, ROIC, governance, capital allocation, economic sensitivity, currencies, valuations, and market structure.

**A lower P/E does not automatically mean better value** — it usually means a different sector mix, a different governance regime, or a different currency.

## Indonesia-specific

For IDX securities, pay particular attention to liquidity, free float, controlling shareholders, related-party transactions, governance, IDR exposure, foreign flows, corporate actions, dividend policy, and commodity sensitivity.

**Treat governance as a valuation input.** For Shariah-sensitive investing, verify current index eligibility rather than assuming it.

---

## Position sizing

Never size from conviction alone. Consider expected return, downside, thesis quality, balance-sheet risk, liquidity, cyclicality, valuation risk, uncertainty, portfolio correlation, and horizon.

```text
                 Conviction × Asymmetry × Thesis Durability
Position Size  ∝  ────────────────────────────────────────────
                 Downside Severity × Uncertainty × Correlation
```

This is a way of thinking, not a literal formula.

## Entry discipline

Before buying, define the required expected return, acceptable valuation, starting size, maximum size, conditions for adding, and conditions for waiting.

**Do not buy simply because the business is attractive.**

## Adding to a position

Only add when expected return has improved, the thesis remains intact, evidence supports the conviction, and concentration remains acceptable.

**A lower price alone is not a reason to add.** Determine whether the decline represents an improved opportunity or new adverse information.

## Exit framework

Reduce or exit on:

- **Thesis break** — the business outcome materially diverges from the original thesis.
- **Valuation** — future return no longer compensates for risk.
- **Opportunity cost** — another investment materially dominates on expected return and risk.
- **Portfolio risk** — exposure becomes too concentrated or correlated.

Do not sell because the stock rose, because it fell, because the holding period feels long, or because the position feels boring.
